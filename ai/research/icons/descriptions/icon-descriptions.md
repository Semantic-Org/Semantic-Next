# Icon Visual Descriptions (V2)


## navigation

**arrow-down** — A bold downward-pointing arrow with a straight vertical shaft and a wide triangular arrowhead at the bottom. The stroke is thick and the arrowhead has flat horizontal ends on each side, giving it a T-bar quality.
**arrow-left** — A bold leftward-pointing arrow with a straight horizontal shaft and a wide triangular arrowhead on the left side. The arrowhead has a flat vertical termination at top and bottom, giving it a broad, blocky appearance. The stroke weight is thick throughout, making it visually prominent.
**arrow-left-right** — Two horizontal arrows pointing in opposite directions: one pointing left on top and one pointing right below it, both with filled arrowheads. The pair is stacked vertically and suggests bidirectional movement.
**arrow-right** — A bold rightward-pointing arrow with a straight horizontal shaft and a wide triangular arrowhead on the right side. The arrowhead has a flat vertical termination at top and bottom, giving it a broad, blocky appearance. The thick stroke weight makes it visually prominent.
**arrow-up** — A bold upward-pointing arrow with a straight vertical shaft and a wide triangular arrowhead at the top. The arrowhead forms a flat horizontal base at the bottom where it meets the shaft, and comes to a point at the top. The thick stroke weight gives it strong visual presence.
**arrow-up-down** — Two arrows stacked vertically: one pointing up on the left and one pointing down on the right, with both arrowheads filled. The pair suggests bidirectional vertical movement or sorting.
**chevron-down** — A thin downward-pointing chevron drawn as two short diagonal strokes of equal length meeting at a sharp point at the bottom, forming a V shape. There is no shaft — just the open angular chevron. The stroke is lightweight and rounded at the ends.
**chevron-first** — A thin chevron pointing left with a vertical bar (pipe) immediately to its left. The chevron is a single open angle, and the vertical bar acts as a boundary stop, together forming a skip-to-start symbol.
**chevron-last** — A thin rightward-pointing chevron (two diagonal strokes meeting at a rightward point) paired with a vertical bar (pipe) immediately to its right. Together they form a skip-to-end or fast-forward-to-last symbol. The chevron and vertical bar are drawn with the same lightweight stroke.
**chevron-left** — A thin single chevron pointing left, drawn as two diagonal strokes of equal length meeting at a sharp leftward point, forming a sideways V or greater-than shape opening right. There is no shaft or decorative bar — just the open angular chevron with a lightweight rounded stroke.
**chevron-right** — A thin single chevron pointing right, drawn as two diagonal strokes of equal length meeting at a sharp rightward point, forming a sideways V or less-than shape opening left. There is no shaft or decorative bar — just the open angular chevron with a lightweight rounded stroke.
**chevron-up** — A thin upward-pointing chevron drawn as two short diagonal strokes of equal length meeting at a sharp point at the top, forming an inverted V or caret shape. There is no shaft — just the open angular chevron. The stroke is lightweight and rounded at the ends.
**chevrons-down-up** — Two thin chevrons stacked vertically and pointing toward each other — one pointing down on top and one pointing up below — creating a compressed or pinch symbol.
**chevrons-left-right** — Two thin chevrons placed side by side horizontally and pointing away from each other — one pointing left on the left and one pointing right on the right — suggesting horizontal expansion.
**chevrons-up-down** — Two thin chevrons stacked vertically and pointing away from each other — one pointing up on top and one pointing down below — suggesting vertical expansion or a sort selector.
**compass** — A circle outline containing a small filled diamond (rotated square) at its center. The compass-rose shape of the diamond inside the ring gives this a directional exploration feel.
**corner-down-left** — A bent arrow that travels rightward then curves downward and left, forming an L-shaped return path with a leftward arrowhead at the end. Represents a reply or return action.
**corner-down-right** — A bent arrow that travels rightward from a lower-left origin, curves up and right along an L-shape, with a rightward arrowhead at the end. Represents a forward redirect or sub-item indentation.
**crosshair** — A circle outline with four small tick marks at the top, bottom, left, and right positions (like compass points), forming a crosshair or targeting reticle inside the ring.
**external-link** — A square with an open corner at the top-right, with an arrow pointing diagonally outward toward the upper-right from inside the square. Represents opening content in a new tab or external window.
**home** — A house outline with a triangular pitched roof, a rectangular base, and a small rectangular door cutout at the bottom center. Clean, minimal stroke with no fill.
**layout-panel-left** — A layout grid showing one tall narrow rectangle on the left spanning the full height, and two smaller squares stacked on the right side. Represents a sidebar-left with content panel layout.
**layout-panel-top** — A layout grid showing one wide rectangle spanning the full top, and two smaller squares side-by-side in the bottom row. Represents a top header with two-column content layout.
**menu** — Three horizontal parallel lines of equal length and equal spacing, stacked vertically. Classic hamburger menu symbol with no additional decoration.
**mouse-pointer** — A filled arrow cursor pointing toward the upper-left, shaped like a traditional mouse pointer with a sharp tip at the top-left and a notched tail. The cursor is solid black with a slight angular cutout at the bottom-right of the body.

## action

**archive** — A rectangular archive box with a flat lid and a small horizontal slot or handle cutout centered on the front face. The box body sits below a separate flat top panel, resembling a storage bin or document box.
**archive-restore** — A wide rectangular box with a thick horizontal lid across the top and squared lower corners, drawn in outline style. The lid has a narrow raised lip along its top edge. From inside the box, an upward-pointing arrow rises through the open top, indicating content being lifted out of storage.
**archive-x** — A rectangular archive box with a flat lid containing an X symbol centered on the front face instead of a slot. The X clearly marks the box for deletion or rejection.
**arrow-down-a-z** — A downward-pointing arrow on the left side alongside the letters A (top) and Z (bottom) stacked on the right, indicating descending alphabetical order from A down to Z.
**arrow-up-a-z** — An upward-pointing arrow on the left side alongside the letters A (top) and Z (bottom) stacked on the right, indicating ascending or reverse sort direction where the arrow points up rather than down.
**bookmark** — A bookmark ribbon shape — a tall rounded rectangle with a downward V-notch cut into the bottom center, making it look like a classic page marker. The shape is outline-only with no interior symbols.
**bookmark-check** — A bookmark ribbon shape — a tall rounded-corner rectangle with a V-shaped notch cut into the bottom center, giving it the classic pointed bookmark silhouette — drawn with an outline stroke. Inside the upper portion of the ribbon, a checkmark is rendered, indicating a confirmed or active saved state.
**bookmark-plus** — A bookmark ribbon shape — a tall rounded-corner rectangle with a V-shaped notch cut into the bottom center, giving it the classic pointed bookmark silhouette — drawn with an outline stroke. Inside the upper portion of the ribbon, a plus sign is rendered, signaling the action of adding a new bookmark.
**circle-ellipsis** — A circle outline containing three small filled dots arranged horizontally in the center, evenly spaced. The dots are contained within the circular border.
**circle-minus** — A circle outline containing a single short horizontal dash or minus sign centered within it. The minus sits alone inside the circular border.
**circle-plus** — A circle outline containing a plus sign centered within it, formed by one vertical and one horizontal stroke crossing at the center.
**circle-x** — A circle outline containing an X formed by two diagonal strokes crossing at the center. The X is enclosed within the circular border.
**clipboard** — A clipboard shape — a tall rounded rectangle representing a clipboard board — with a small rectangular clip tab at the top center. The interior is empty with no content lines.
**clipboard-paste** — A clipboard shape with the small top clip tab, and a right-pointing arrow overlaid in the lower right area of the clipboard body, suggesting content being sent or pasted out from the clipboard.
**cloud-download** — A cloud outline with a downward-pointing arrow hanging below the cloud and pointing into a small horizontal base line or tray. The arrow descends from the bottom center of the cloud.
**cloud-upload** — A cloud outline with an upward-pointing arrow rising from within the cloud toward the top center. The arrow points upward out of or into the cloud body.
**copy** — Two overlapping rounded rectangles — one slightly behind and offset to the upper-left, one in the foreground — representing a copy or duplicate action. Both rectangles are outline only with no interior content.
**copy-check** — Two overlapping rounded rectangles arranged in an offset stack — a slightly smaller foreground rectangle sits in front of and above-right of a background rectangle — drawn with outline strokes. Inside the foreground rectangle, a checkmark is rendered, indicating the copy action was completed successfully.
**backspace** — A leftward-pointing pentagon or arrow-tab shape (like a speech bubble pointing left) containing an X formed by two diagonal crossing strokes inside it. The overall shape resembles a backspace key.
**download** — A downward-pointing arrow with a horizontal baseline below it — the arrow descends from above and its tip points into or rests on the flat line at the bottom. The baseline extends left and right below the arrowhead.
**ellipsis** — Three solid filled circles arranged in a horizontal row with equal spacing between them. The circles are larger and bolder than typical dots, with no enclosing border or container.
**ellipsis-vertical** — Three solid filled circles arranged in a vertical column with equal spacing between them, stacked top to bottom. No enclosing border or container.
**expand** — Four diagonal arrows pointing outward toward each of the four corners — upper-left, upper-right, lower-left, lower-right — creating a symmetrical expand or fullscreen symbol.
**eye** — An eye shape — a wide oval outline forming the eye surround — with a smaller circle (iris/pupil) centered within it. The overall icon is outline-only and horizontally elongated.
**eye-off** — An eye shape with a diagonal line (slash) crossing through it from lower-left to upper-right, obscuring the eye and indicating the hidden or invisible state.
**grip-horizontal** — Six solid filled circles arranged in a 2-column by 3-row grid (two columns, three rows), evenly spaced. The grid is wider than tall and resembles a drag handle or app grid.
**grip-vertical** — Six solid filled circles arranged in a 2-column by 3-row grid, evenly spaced. The overall arrangement is taller than it is wide, with the two columns of dots producing a vertical drag-handle appearance. The dots are bold and clearly filled.
**hand** — An open hand with four fingers extended upward and thumb slightly out to the side, drawn as a bold outline shape. The hand faces forward as if signaling stop or requesting attention.
**heart** — A heart shape drawn as a smooth outline, formed by two rounded lobes at the top meeting at a downward point at the bottom. The interior is empty with no fill.
**heart-off** — A heart shape with a diagonal line (slash) crossing through it from lower-left to upper-right, negating the heart symbol. The slash breaks through the heart outline.
**link** — Two interlocking chain links arranged diagonally, forming the classic hyperlink symbol. The links are rendered as thick rounded rectangles overlapping at the center.
**list-checks** — A checklist with two rows, each showing a bold checkmark on the left and a short horizontal line representing text on the right. The checkmarks are large and the lines are medium-length.
**maximize** — Two diagonal arrows pointing away from each other — one toward the upper-right and one toward the lower-left — connected by a dashed line in the middle, indicating expansion or fullscreen.
**merge** — A single upward-pointing arrow whose shaft splits into two branches diverging to the lower-left and lower-right, resembling a merge or fork shape pointing upward.
**minimize** — Two diagonal arrows pointing toward each other — one from the upper-right and one from the lower-left — both aimed at the center, indicating compression or collapse.
**minus** — A single short, thick horizontal line centered in the icon with rounded ends, representing a minus or remove symbol.
**mouse-pointer-click** — A cursor arrow pointer angled to the lower-left with short radiating lines around its tip, suggesting a click or tap action with visual feedback.
**move** — Four arrows pointing outward in all four cardinal directions (up, down, left, right) from a shared center point, forming a cross or move symbol.
**paperclip** — A paperclip shape with a looping oval form tapering to a point at the bottom, rendered in the classic diagonal paperclip orientation.
**pencil** — A pencil or pen shape oriented diagonally from lower-left to upper-right, with a pointed tip at the lower-left and a flat eraser end at the upper-right.
**pin** — A pushpin with a round head at the top and a narrow vertical stem pointing downward, viewed from the front with the pin centered and symmetric.
**pin-off** — A pushpin shape with a diagonal line crossing through it from upper-left to lower-right, indicating a disabled or negated pin state.
**plus** — A large plus sign with four equal arms of uniform thickness, centered in the icon, representing add or create.
**redo** — A curved arrow that arcs to the left and then turns sharply to point right, resembling a forward-redirect or redo shape with the arrowhead on the right side.
**refresh** — Two circular arrows forming a complete refresh loop — one arc on the upper-left curving clockwise and one on the lower-right, with arrowheads at both tips pointing in opposite directions.
**replace** — A small square in the lower-left with a downward-pointing arrow above it and a dotted/dashed partial square shape in the upper-right, suggesting an item being dragged or dropped into place.
**rocket** — A rocket ship silhouette pointing toward the upper-right, with a rounded body, a pointed nose cone, and a small flame or exhaust detail at the lower-left tail.
**rotate-ccw** — A circular arrow forming almost a full circle with the arrowhead on the left side pointing counterclockwise, representing undo or rotate-left.
**rotate-cw** — A circular arrow forming almost a full circle with the arrowhead on the right side pointing clockwise, representing redo or rotate-right.
**save** — A floppy disk shape with a square body, a rectangular cutout at the top-right corner for the label area, and a smaller rectangle centered at the bottom representing the disk slot.
**scan** — Four right-angle corner brackets at each of the four corners of an imaginary rectangle, with open space in the center, representing a fullscreen or focus frame.
**scissors** — A pair of scissors with two circular handle loops on the left and two open blades extending to the upper-right and lower-right, in the classic cut symbol orientation.
**search** — A magnifying glass with a circular lens on the upper-left and a short handle extending to the lower-right, representing search or zoom.
**share** — An upward-pointing arrow emerging from a tray or box shape at the bottom — the box has an open top and the arrow points straight up out of it, representing an upload or share-from action.
**split** — A single vertical arrow pointing downward that splits into two branches diverging upward-left and upward-right, resembling an upside-down fork or a split/branch shape.
**thumbs-down** — A hand with the thumb pointing downward and fingers curled, the classic thumbs-down or dislike gesture, viewed at a slight angle.
**thumbs-up** — A hand with the thumb pointing upward and fingers curled forward, the classic thumbs-up or like gesture, viewed at a slight angle.
**trash** — A trash can with a rectangular body, a flat lid at the top with a small handle tab, and two vertical lines inside the body representing slots or dividers.
**undo** — A curved arrow that arcs upward to the right and then turns to point back to the left, resembling a reply or undo shape with the arrowhead on the left side.
**unlink** — Two curved arc segments breaking apart from each other — one in the upper-right and one in the lower-left — with small radiating tick marks at the break points, suggesting a chain or link being severed.
**upload** — An upward-pointing arrow emerging from a tray or inbox shape — a thick arrow pointing up sits above a rectangular container open at the top, resembling a document being lifted out of a slot.
**vote** — A laptop computer outline with a checkmark inside the screen area — the lid and keyboard base are shown in profile, and a bold tick mark fills the screen cavity.
**x** — A large X formed by two diagonal lines crossing at the center of the icon, with rounded or slightly tapered ends on each line stroke.
**zoom-in** — A magnifying glass with a plus sign inside the circular lens — the handle extends to the lower right, and the plus symbol is centered within the circle.
**zoom-out** — A circular lens with a short horizontal dash (minus sign) centered inside it, attached to a diagonal handle extending from the lower-right of the circle toward the bottom-right corner. The handle is slightly thicker than the circle's outline stroke.

## status

**badge-alert** — A badge-shaped outline (scalloped/starburst edge) with a bold exclamation mark centered inside. The scalloped border has multiple small rounded lobes forming the perimeter, distinguishing it from a plain circle.
**badge-check** — A rounded outline shape with a continuous scalloped or pinched edge forming roughly 8–10 small inward curves around the perimeter, giving the appearance of a decorative seal or starburst badge. A bold checkmark is centered inside the badge, with the tick's left leg shorter and the right leg longer, angled upward.
**badge-info** — A badge-shaped outline (scalloped/starburst edge) with a lowercase letter 'i' (information symbol) centered inside. The scalloped border matches the other badge icons; the interior contains only the info 'i' glyph.
**ban** — A plain circle with a diagonal line crossing through it from upper-right to lower-left (prohibition/no-entry symbol). There is no other interior symbol — just the circle and the single diagonal stroke.
**check** — A standalone check mark (tick) with no enclosing shape. The stroke curves slightly at the base of the short left arm and the longer right arm sweeps upward to the right.
**check-check** — Two overlapping check marks (double tick), both angled the same direction, with the second tick offset slightly to the right of and behind the first. No enclosing shape.
**error** — A circle outline with a bold exclamation mark centered inside. Unlike the scalloped badge version, the border is a smooth perfect circle with no lobes.
**circle-check** — A circle outline with a small check mark centered inside. The border is a smooth perfect circle (not scalloped), and the check mark is relatively small compared to the circle's interior.
**circle-dashed** — A dashed circle made up of evenly spaced short strokes arranged in a ring, with no interior symbol. The gaps between dashes are consistent, giving the appearance of an incomplete or in-progress ring.
**help** — A circle outline with a question mark centered inside. The border is a smooth perfect circle, and the question mark includes both the curved upper portion and the dot below.
**construction** — A rectangular barrier or roadblock icon: a wide horizontal rectangle with diagonal stripes (hatching) across its face, supported by two vertical posts at each end that extend below the bar. Resembles a construction barricade.
**flag** — A flag icon with a waving rectangular flag on a vertical pole. The flag curves slightly at the right edge suggesting motion, and the pole is a straight vertical line on the left. The flag is outlined without fill.
**flame** — A flame icon showing a single teardrop-shaped fire with a curved inner highlight stroke suggesting the flame's inner core. The silhouette tapers to a point at the top and widens at the base.
**frown** — A circle outline containing a simple sad face: two small dots for eyes positioned in the upper half, and a downward-curved arc for a frown in the lower half.
**info** — A smooth, perfectly round circle outline containing a lowercase letter 'i' centered inside. The 'i' consists of a small filled dot above a short vertical stroke, both centered on the circle's vertical axis.
**life-buoy** — A circle with a smaller circle centered inside it and four lines extending outward from the inner circle toward the outer ring at the four diagonal (45°) positions, dividing the ring into quadrants. Resembles a life preserver or crosshair.
**loader** — A loading spinner made of eight short rounded strokes radiating outward from the center like spokes on a wheel, evenly spaced at 45° intervals. The strokes vary in opacity or weight suggesting rotation direction.
**loader-circle** — A thin arc forming approximately three-quarters of a circle, open at the lower-right, with rounded endpoints. It looks like an incomplete circle or a 'C' rotated slightly — commonly associated with a spinning loader.
**meh** — A circle outline containing a neutral face: two small dots for eyes and a straight horizontal line for a mouth, expressing neither happiness nor sadness.
**server-crash** — A rounded rectangle outline (like a badge or ID card shape) containing a bold lightning bolt symbol centered inside, with two small circles in the lower-left and lower-right corners of the rectangle.
**warning** — A triangle outline with rounded corners pointing upward and a bold exclamation mark centered inside. Unlike the circle-based warning icons, the enclosing shape is clearly triangular — the standard caution/warning symbol shape.
**bolt** — A standalone lightning bolt shape — a thick zigzag stroke that angles sharply downward-right then down-left, with no enclosing shape. The bolt has a slight backward lean at the top.

## user

**avatar** — A person silhouette enclosed within a full circle border. Inside the circle, a small hollow circle represents the head, positioned above a short arc representing the shoulders/torso. The enclosing ring gives it an avatar or profile badge appearance.
**contact** — A person silhouette enclosed within a rounded rectangle border, resembling a contact card or ID badge frame. A small circle for the head sits above a shoulder arc, all contained within the card outline.
**id-card** — A horizontal rounded rectangle (card shape) containing a small person silhouette on the left side, paired with two short horizontal lines on the right representing text rows. The layout explicitly mimics a contact list row or ID card.
**user** — A plain, unenclosed person silhouette: a hollow circle for the head centered above a smooth upward-curving arc representing the shoulders. No border, badge, or additional decoration — the minimal baseline user icon.
**user-check** — A person silhouette (head circle above shoulder arc) with a checkmark badge positioned at the lower-right. The check is drawn in the same stroke weight as the figure and floats just outside the shoulder arc.
**user-cog** — A person silhouette (head circle above shoulder arc) with a small gear/cog icon positioned at the lower-right of the figure. The gear badge clearly signals a settings or configuration action tied to the user.
**user-minus** — A person silhouette (head circle above shoulder arc) with a horizontal minus/dash symbol positioned at the lower-right. The minus badge indicates subtraction or removal of the user.
**user-plus** — A person silhouette (head circle above shoulder arc) with a plus symbol positioned at the lower-right. The plus badge clearly signals adding or inviting a new person.
**user-search** — A person silhouette (head circle above shoulder arc) with a magnifying glass icon positioned at the lower-right. The search badge combined with the figure suggests looking up or finding a specific user.
**user-x** — A person silhouette (head circle above shoulder arc) with an X symbol positioned at the lower-right. The X badge signals deletion, blocking, or dismissal of the user.
**users** — A person silhouette (head circle above shoulder arc) with two small curved concentric arcs emanating from the right side of the head, resembling sound waves or speech lines. The arcs suggest the person is speaking, broadcasting, or active.

## form

**circle-dot** — A large hollow circle with a small filled circle (dot) at its center, representing a radio button in its selected/active state. The outer ring is thick and the inner dot is clearly centered.
**password** — A rounded rectangle (pill-shaped outer border) containing three horizontal dots evenly spaced in a row, resembling a text input field with placeholder dots. The corners are fully rounded giving it a capsule shape.
**square-check** — A square with rounded corners containing a bold checkmark in the lower-left-to-upper-right direction, representing a checked checkbox. The checkmark stroke is thick and clearly visible inside the square.
**text-cursor-input** — A text cursor (I-beam) symbol with two horizontal bars — one at the top and one at the bottom — flanked by curved bracket-like serifs on the left and right sides, indicating an active text input field. The overall shape resembles a capital letter I with wide serifs.
**toggle-left** — A rounded rectangle (toggle switch outline) with a circle positioned on the left side inside it, indicating an off/disabled toggle state. The circle sits flush to the left end of the pill-shaped track.
**toggle-right** — A horizontal pill-shaped outline (wide rounded rectangle) representing a toggle switch track, with a circle positioned inside toward the right end of the pill. The circle sits flush against the right inner edge, indicating the toggle is in the on or enabled position.

## data

**activity** — A waveform or activity monitor line: a flat horizontal line that spikes sharply upward then dips into a V-shape before returning flat, resembling an ECG/heartbeat pulse trace.
**chart-area** — An area chart inside a coordinate axis frame: a filled mountain-like shape rising from the baseline sits within L-shaped axes (bottom and left), representing cumulative or trend area data.
**chart-bar** — A horizontal bar chart with three bars of decreasing length from top to bottom, anchored to a vertical left axis — resembling a left-aligned ranking or comparison list with no bottom axis visible.
**chart-bar-stacked** — A horizontal bar chart where each bar is represented as a rounded pill/capsule shape with two end caps, shown in three rows of varying lengths inside L-shaped axes — resembling a lollipop or segment bar chart.
**chart-candlestick** — A candlestick chart with two vertical candlestick shapes (each with a narrow wick line above and below a taller rectangular body) sitting on L-shaped coordinate axes, representing financial OHLC data.
**chart-gantt** — Three horizontal lines of decreasing length (longest at top, shortest at bottom) anchored to a left vertical axis line, resembling a left-aligned descending bar chart or a sorted list.
**chart-line** — A line chart inside L-shaped axes: a single polyline that drops then rises sharply forming a zigzag W-shape, suggesting a trend line with a dip and recovery.
**chart-network** — A connected node graph inside L-shaped axes: three filled circular nodes connected by straight lines forming a path (lower-left, upper-middle, lower-right), resembling a scatter plot with connected data points.
**chart-pie** — A pie chart with one slice separated or highlighted: the circle has a gap in the upper-right quadrant with a distinct pie wedge detached from the rest, like a donut-style exploded pie chart with an open arc.
**chart-scatter** — A scatter plot inside L-shaped axes: five small filled circles scattered at different positions across the chart area with no connecting lines, representing individual data point distribution.
**clipboard-list** — A clipboard icon: a rectangular document with a small rectangular clip tab at the top center, and three horizontal lines (two short dots followed by a short dash on each row) inside representing a checklist or data list.
**funnel** — A funnel or filter icon: a wide inverted triangle at the top that narrows to a short stem at the bottom, like a classic funnel shape used to represent filtering.
**gauge** — A speedometer or gauge icon: a semicircle arc open at the bottom with a short diagonal needle line pointing to the upper-left, like a dashboard gauge at a low reading.
**grid-2x2** — A 2x2 grid of rounded squares inside a larger rounded-corner square border, with all four cells equal in size and evenly separated by a cross divider — a compact four-cell table or tile layout.
**grid-3x3** — A 3x3 grid of rounded squares inside a larger rounded-corner square border, forming nine equal cells arranged in three rows and three columns — a dense grid or table layout.
**layout-dashboard** — Four rounded squares arranged in a 2x2 layout but with varying sizes: top-left is small, top-right is wider, bottom-left is taller, bottom-right is smaller — a dashboard bento-box or mosaic grid.
**layout-grid** — Four equal rounded squares arranged in a symmetrical 2x2 grid with uniform spacing and no outer border, creating a clean four-tile layout with visible gaps between each square.
**layout-list** — Two rows of content: each row has a small rounded square on the left followed by two horizontal lines of text to the right, creating a list-with-thumbnail layout.
**list-filter** — Three horizontal lines of decreasing length centered below each other (longest at top, shortest at bottom), with no axes or chart elements — a simple centered text alignment or sort/filter icon.
**list-tree** — A hierarchical outline or tree list: a short vertical stem on the left branches into three horizontal lines at different indentation levels, suggesting a nested or parent-child data structure.
**qr-code** — A QR code icon: a stylized square QR pattern with three corner finder squares (rounded), inner dot patterns, and the characteristic mix of small squares that form a scannable code.
**radar** — A radar or sonar icon: three concentric arcs of increasing size centered on a lower-left point, with a small filled circle at the center origin — resembling a signal emission or target radar sweep.
**sigma** — The Greek capital letter sigma (Σ) displayed as a mathematical symbol, with a wide flat top, a diagonal stroke going down-right, then mirrored back up — the standard summation symbol.
**table** — A rounded-corner square border containing a 2x2 internal grid created by one horizontal line and one vertical line dividing the interior into four equal cells, with a header row across the top separated by the horizontal line. The result is a compact table with a bold outer border and clear internal grid lines.
**table-cells-merge** — A table layout with a distinct header row: a rounded-corner square border containing a top row that is taller/bolder than the two equal rows below it, forming a 1+2 row structure with two columns.
**table-cells-split** — A table layout with a large merged top row spanning the full width, and a second row split into two equal cells below it inside a rounded-corner border — a two-row layout with a spanning header.
**trending-down** — A bold downward-trending arrow: a thick polyline that starts at the upper-left, steps down at a right angle, then continues down-left before ending with a solid arrowhead pointing to the lower-right — a declining trend indicator.
**trending-up** — A bold upward-trending arrow: a thick polyline that starts at the lower-left, zigzags up with a V-dip in the middle, then rises and ends with a solid arrowhead pointing to the upper-right — a rising trend indicator.

## file

**file** — A plain document outline with a folded upper-right corner, containing no interior content. The clean silhouette uses just the page shape and dog-ear fold.
**file-archive** — A document with a folded upper-right corner and a vertical ellipsis (three stacked dots) in the center, with a small circle below it resembling a version-control node. Suggests a file with additional options or a commit point.
**file-check** — A document with a folded upper-right corner and a bold checkmark in the lower-center area. The check visually signals approval or completion.
**file-code** — A document with a folded upper-right corner and a pair of angle brackets (< >) centered inside, representing source code. The symbols are distinctly code-like.
**file-image** — A document with a folded upper-right corner containing a small circle with a landscape/mountain image placeholder in the lower area. Represents an image embedded in or attached to a file.
**file-lock** — A document with a folded upper-right corner and a padlock icon centered in the lower half. The lock clearly signals restricted or protected access.
**file-plus** — A document with a folded upper-right corner and a bold plus sign (+) centered inside. The plus signals adding or creating.
**file-search** — A document with a folded upper-right corner and a magnifying glass icon centered inside. The lens overlaps the lower portion of the page.
**file-spreadsheet** — A document with a folded upper-right corner containing three pairs of short horizontal dashes arranged in rows, resembling a structured list or spreadsheet grid.
**file-text** — A document outline with a folded upper-right corner — the top-right of the rectangle is cut diagonally and the folded flap is shown as a small triangular crease. Inside the document body, two or three short horizontal lines represent lines of text, centered and stacked vertically.
**file-x** — A document with a folded upper-right corner and a bold X (cross) centered inside. The X clearly signals removal or error.
**files** — Two overlapping document outlines — a smaller page in front and a slightly larger one behind and to the left — both with folded upper-right corners, representing duplication.
**folder** — A simple open folder outline — a rectangular body with a slightly raised tab on the top-left — with no contents visible. The folder is closed-looking but rendered with rounded corners.
