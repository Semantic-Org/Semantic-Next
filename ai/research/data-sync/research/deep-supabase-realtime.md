# Reference Dossier — Supabase Realtime

Deep research on Supabase Realtime for the data-sync reference comparison, 2026-06-12. Compares against [`plan.md`](../plan.md), [`ws-protocol.md`](../ws-protocol.md), [`scenario.md`](../scenario.md), [`scenario-hobbyist.md`](../scenario-hobbyist.md). The apples-to-oranges pick on purpose: Supabase Realtime is not a sync engine — it is a channel/broadcast/presence layer operated at massive multi-tenant scale, the closest production mirror of our channel model and ephemeral tier. Its lessons price our router economics, not our store.

Framing discipline: no "X wins." Each finding lands as adopt / reconsider / confirmed-divergence against a named spec section and the scenario requirement that makes it matter.

## 2. Architecture

**Storage**: none for Broadcast/Presence in the durable sense. Presence is "an in-memory key-value store backed by a CRDT" (Phoenix Tracker), state replicated to all nodes. Broadcast-from-database messages land in `realtime.messages`, **partitioned by day, partitions retained 3 days then dropped** — retention by partition-drop, not row deletion. Postgres Changes reads a logical replication slot. ([architecture](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/architecture))

**Transport**: WebSocket via Phoenix Channels. REST endpoint for fire-and-forget broadcast without a socket. Binary payloads recent (supabase-js 2.91.0+) — older clients **silently drop** binary frames, their own compat lesson in miniature. ([broadcast docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/broadcast))

**Cluster topology** (from source): globally distributed Elixir cluster, libcluster for formation (they wrote [libcluster_postgres](https://github.com/supabase/libcluster_postgres) — cluster discovery through the database they already have, [blog](https://supabase.com/blog/elixir-clustering-using-postgres)). Cross-node messaging is **not stock distributed Erlang**: `application.ex` starts Phoenix.PubSub with a custom `Realtime.GenRpcPubSub` adapter over [gen_rpc](https://github.com/priestjim/gen_rpc) — per-node TCP connection pools (separate cast/call pools), sharding traffic across ports instead of the single distributed-Erlang port (head-of-line blocking at scale). An optional `key` parameter gives per-key cross-node ordering. A `Forum.Census` partitioned user-tracking supervisor and a `ForumPubSubAdapter` doing "regional broadcasting which is more efficient in this multi-region setup" are newer, sparsely documented. ([application.ex](https://github.com/supabase/realtime/blob/main/lib/realtime/application.ex), [gen_rpc.ex](https://github.com/supabase/realtime/blob/main/lib/realtime/gen_rpc.ex), [forum_pub_sub_adapter.ex](https://github.com/supabase/realtime/blob/main/lib/realtime/forum_pub_sub_adapter.ex))

**Multi-tenancy** (from source): every limit is per-tenant and enforced in the channel join path — the `join/3` `with` chain checks shutdown state, tenant existence, private-only mode, `limit_max_users`, `limit_joins` (rate counter), `limit_channels` (Registry count per transport pid) before any auth work. One database connection per tenant, resolved through a `:syn` global registry (`Connect.lookup_or_start_connection(tenant_id)`). Per-tenant rate counters via Cachex + GenCounter; a periodic `:update_rate_counter` check **shuts the channel down** when events/sec exceeds the tenant cap. ([realtime_channel.ex](https://github.com/supabase/realtime/blob/main/lib/realtime_web/channels/realtime_channel.ex))

**Partial sync**: none. Channels carry whatever is published into them. No projections, no per-field anything — Broadcast payloads are app-defined blobs, Postgres Changes carries the row (column choice only via the trigger's `broadcast_changes` arguments).

**Conflict**: none. No write path of its own — writes go to Postgres through the normal API, Realtime is delivery only.

**Backend coupling**: deep to Postgres for Postgres Changes and broadcast-from-database (replication slots, publications, RLS), zero for plain Broadcast/Presence (pure message bus).

## 3. The Famous Paid-For Lesson: Postgres Changes → Broadcast from Database

This is the center of the dossier — the production mirror of our Decision #8 (write-time invalidation routing) and our permission-at-subscribe posture.

**Where the ceiling was.** Postgres Changes was v1's entire product: WAL → Elixir decode → fan to subscribers. Securing it meant applying RLS per row per subscriber. The WALRUS announcement states the requirement they accepted: "We recognized that to fully secure Realtime in accordance with row level security, a row's visibility must be checked separately for each user on every change." Their response was to make the per-subscriber check as fast as possible — WALRUS runs inside Postgres (a security-definer function assumes each subscriber's identity and probes visibility by primary key, prepared statements, no network I/O). Published scaling numbers, linear in subscribers per change: **11.2ms at 1 subscriber, 24.5ms at 100, 303.8ms at 10,000**. ([WALRUS blog](https://supabase.com/blog/realtime-row-level-security-in-postgresql), [walrus repo](https://github.com/supabase/walrus))

**The second constraint**: "Database changes are processed on a single thread to maintain the change order. That means compute upgrades don't have a large effect on the performance of Postgres change subscriptions." ([benchmarks](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/benchmarks)) And the multiplicative read load: "If you have 100 users subscribed to a table where you make a single insert, it will then trigger 100 'reads': one for each user." ([postgres-changes docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/postgres-changes))

**What they tell users today, verbatim**: Broadcast is "the recommended method for scalability and security"; Postgres Changes "is a simpler method. It requires less setup, but does not scale as well as Broadcast"; "use Broadcast for most use cases." For those staying on Postgres Changes at scale: "you should consider using separate 'public' table without RLS and filters. Alternatively, you can use Realtime server-side only and then re-stream the changes to your clients using a Realtime Broadcast." ([subscribing-to-database-changes](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/subscribing-to-database-changes), [postgres-changes](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/postgres-changes))

**What Broadcast-from-database does instead**: a trigger calls `realtime.broadcast_changes()` (or `realtime.send()`) which inserts into `realtime.messages` with an app-chosen topic (`topic:${id}`); Realtime holds one replication slot against that single table and fans by topic. Authorization moved from per-change-per-subscriber to **once at channel join** (RLS on `realtime.messages`, evaluated by insert-and-rollback, cached for the connection). Scale claim: "tens of thousands of connected users at once." ([broadcast-from-database blog](https://supabase.com/blog/realtime-broadcast-from-database))

**Read against our spec**: this is our architecture, arrived at by paying the alternative's full price first. The trigger naming a topic at write time *is* write-time channel routing (plan, Decision #8 / Routing and membership transitions) retrofitted onto a database via triggers. Auth-once-at-join *is* permission-at-subscribe (plan, Security Posture). The single shared replication slot feeding topic fan-out *is* "CDC lands once and selector-matches through the shared delta log," and their pathology was exactly the one the plan names: per-subscriber processing of an unfiltered change stream. The strongest external validation in this comparison set — from the one team that operated both designs at fleet scale.

A nuance worth keeping: their migration forced **user-visible API change** (different client API, triggers to write, policies to add — docs say "requires more initial setup"). Our liveness tiers and `ephemeral` flag claim graduation without rewrite. Their history is the cautionary tale for shipping the wrong default first.

## 4. Axes Our Spec Lives On

### Delta / field granularity
None. Broadcast payloads are opaque app JSON. `broadcast_changes` can include chosen columns or the whole record — per-table, in the trigger, not per-channel projection and never per-field deltas. Our field-granular wire (`ws-protocol` §2 `delta.changes` paths) has no analog here. Confirmed-divergence by product scope: they ship a bus, we ship a replica.

### Subscription topology + dedup
Channel = topic string, shared by all subscribers of that topic — same instance-sharing economics as our `(name, args)` channel address (ws-protocol §1). Their dedup at dispatch (from [message_dispatcher.ex](https://github.com/supabase/realtime/blob/main/lib/realtime_web/channels/realtime_channel/message_dispatcher.ex)): a custom Phoenix dispatch that **caches the serialized message per `{serializer, join_topic}` and reuses the encoding for every fastlane subscriber** — serialize once, fan N ways, with per-subscriber context (tenant, log level, replayed-ids) packed into subscription metadata at registration so dispatch never touches per-subscriber channel processes. Production proof of the plan's "one selector match per write and one delta fanned 500 ways" and ws-protocol's no-per-subscriber-fields-in-fan-out-frames invariant.

No server-side refcount of client subscriptions beyond the per-socket set — same posture as ws-protocol §1 (client-side refcounting, server per-socket sub set is a set).

### Fan-out economics + backpressure as product surface
The quota table is the economics made legible ([quotas](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/quotas)):

| Quota | Free | Pro | Pro no-cap / Team | Enterprise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Concurrent connections | 200 | 500 | 10,000 | 10,000+ |
| Messages/sec | 100 | 500 | 2,500 | 2,500+ |
| Channel joins/sec | 100 | 500 | 2,500 | 2,500+ |
| Channels per connection | 100 | 100 | 100 | 100+ |
| Presence keys per object | 10 | 10 | 10 | 10+ |
| Presence messages/sec | 20 | 50 | 1,000 | 1,000+ |
| Presence track calls per client | 5 per 30s | 5 per 30s | 5 per 30s | 5 per 30s |
| Broadcast payload | 256KB | 3MB | 3MB | 3MB+ |
| Postgres Changes payload | 1MB | 1MB | 1MB | 1MB+ |

Violations surface as named errors (`too_many_channels`, `too_many_connections`, `too_many_joins`, `tenant_events`) and **automatic disconnection, with reconnection accepted once rates drop below plan limits**. In source, exceeding the events/sec counter shuts the channel down with a message. Backpressure here is quota + disconnect — there is no catch-up story because there is no log; a slow consumer just misses messages (at-most-once absorbs everything). Our reset-as-pressure-valve (ws-protocol §4) is the log-bearing analog: both refuse per-client buffering, the named anti-pattern in both designs (their fire-and-forget, our rejected Socket.IO mini-mergebox).

Published benchmark ceilings ([benchmarks](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/benchmarks)): WebSocket broadcast 32k concurrent users / 224k msgs/sec / 6ms median, p95 28ms; large-scale 250k users / 800k+ msgs/sec at 100 users per channel / 58ms median, p99 508ms; broadcast-with-auth 50k users / 150k+ msgs/sec; database broadcast 80k users / 10k msgs/sec / 46ms median. Postgres Changes: no headline numbers published at all — the caveats section stands where the benchmark would be.

### Delivery guarantees, ordering, healing
- **At-most-once, by design.** Maintainer framing in [discussion #21093](https://github.com/orgs/supabase/discussions/21093): fire-and-forget — read WAL, broadcast to connected clients, move on; nothing is persisted for the disconnected. Named loss scenarios: WAL buildup exhausting disk (database crash), replication lag exceeding memory forcing a fresh slot (changes permanently skipped), client disconnection during events (no queue).
- **Healing pattern, official**: "use Realtime for instant updates when connected, but on reconnect always fetch current state from the database. Don't rely on the event stream being complete." Users have asked for better ([issue #1010](https://github.com/supabase/realtime/issues/1010), [discussion #5641](https://github.com/orgs/supabase/discussions/5641)) since 2022.
- **Ordering**: Postgres Changes is single-threaded specifically to preserve order. Broadcast is best-effort — Phoenix gives per-channel ordering on a node, the cluster does not guarantee it (gen_rpc's `key` option exists because ordering otherwise sharded away).
- **The walk-back in progress**: **Broadcast Replay** (new, private channels only) — `since` (epoch ms, required) + `limit` (max 25), serving only database-sourced messages from the 3-day partitions. ([broadcast docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/broadcast)) This is a cursor and a tail-replay growing inside an at-most-once system, one feature at a time. Strongest evidence in this dossier that our cursor-core (Decision #7, ws-protocol §5) is the right spine for a *data* product: they are backfilling it after the fact, capped at 25 messages, for one message source.

Against the scenario: at-most-once + refetch-on-reconnect cannot meet scenario.md's failure taxonomy for the detail channel (a missed delta on `records.byId` is unbounded staleness on an implicit-save surface — soft fail with no ceiling, undisclosed). Their context genuinely differs: millions of tenant channels where per-channel logs are the cost they refuse, and the database is always there to refetch from. Confirmed-divergence, with their replay feature as the corroborating footnote.

### Offline posture + outbox/rebase
None and none. No client write path (writes go through PostgREST/the app's API), so no outbox, no optimistic apply, no rebase. The client heals by refetching. Out of scope for them; our whole upstream half (plan, Write Path / Sync Loop) has no counterpart here.

### Boot + reconnect choreography
Join is the heavy moment (auth policies computed at join, capped by joins/sec quotas); reconnect is rejoin — no positions, no resume (Broadcast Replay excepted, opt-in, 25-message window). supabase-js auto-reconnects with backoff but "loses any updates on server during this time" (community-documented, [discussion #5641](https://github.com/orgs/supabase/discussions/5641)). Background-tab silent disconnections are a documented troubleshooting page ([silent disconnections](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/troubleshooting/realtime-handling-silent-disconnections-in-backgrounded-applications-592794)) — clients are told to re-track presence and re-subscribe on `visibilitychange`. Our §3 visibility short-circuit and §5 resume choreography answer the same physics with client-held cursors.

### Schema + validation
None. Payloads are unvalidated JSON. (Postgres Changes payloads inherit table shape; size cap 1MB with larger payloads dropped to a notice.) No analog of our schema/doc-gate (plan, Schemas) — different product layer.

### Permissions
([authorization docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/authorization), source `maybe_assign_policies`)
- Channels are public or `private: true`. Private join evaluates **RLS policies on `realtime.messages`** — Realtime performs a representative query/insert against the table **and rolls it back**; the table stores nothing for this purpose. SELECT policy = may receive (read), INSERT policy = may send (write), discriminated by `realtime.messages.extension` (`broadcast` | `presence`).
- **Granularity is per-join, not per-message**: "The validation is done when the user connects… their permissions are calculated based on" JWT claims, headers, and topic. Policies cache in `socket.assigns.policies` for the connection. Inbound frames check the cached policy (source: messages silently dropped with a warning when `BroadcastPolicies{read: false}`) — no database touch per message.
- **Refresh**: a new JWT via the `access_token` message **resets the policy cache to nil and re-evaluates** (source). "If a new JWT is never received on the Channel, the client will be disconnected when the JWT expires." Revocation latency = token lifetime; docs say keep expiry windows short.
- Performance warning, verbatim: "Increased RLS complexity can impact database performance and connection time, leading to higher connection latency and decreased join rates."

Resonance: this is our two-gates posture (plan, Security Posture — authorize at subscribe and at call, frames carry no permission semantics) converged on independently *after* the per-message-per-subscriber model hit its ceiling. The delta against our spec: ws-protocol §4 says on auth refresh "subscriptions, cursors, and in-flight calls are untouched" — Supabase's flow re-runs channel authorization on token refresh, and their JWT-expiry disconnect is the blunt fallback. Our spec currently has no re-gating moment between subscribe and `server.revoke`.

### Presence / ephemeral tier
([presence docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/presence), [multiplayer GA blog](https://supabase.com/blog/supabase-realtime-multiplayer-general-availability))
- **Internals**: Phoenix Tracker — "a delta-based conflict-free replicated data type (CRDT) for eventually consistent and conflict-free synced state," in-memory, replicated to all nodes. State is latest-per-key (key → array of metas, typically one). Default key is a UUIDv1 per client; custom keys allowed.
- **Join choreography**: new subscriber gets full state (`sync`), then incremental `join`/`leave`. Documented artifact: "During a `sync` event, you may receive `join` and `leave` events simultaneously, even though no users are actually joining or leaving" — CRDT reconciliation leaking into the event API.
- **Scale posture**: presence is the most heavily throttled surface in the product — 5 track calls per client per 30 seconds, 10 keys per object, 20-1,000 presence msgs/sec by plan. Docs, verbatim: "Calling `track()` rapidly — for example on every mouse move to share cursor positions — will flood the channel and cause performance problems." For cursors: "use Broadcast instead." Presence machinery only engages if used (`presence_enabled?` gate in source — a recent optimization).
- **Origin story** (multiplayer GA): "Previously, developers were sending all the mouse movements through their Postgres database which added latency and degraded database performance" — Broadcast and Presence exist because the durable pipeline was catastrophically mispriced for ephemera. The exact thesis of our ephemeral tier (plan, Ephemeral Collections: "the durable pipeline is correct but mispriced by orders of magnitude here").
- Production wart: background-tab throttling leaves ghost presence entries; the fix is client-side re-track on visibility.

Read against our ephemeral collections: they split the tier in two — Presence (merged state, connection-expiry, expensive) and Broadcast (fire-and-forget, cheap, stateless) — and route cursors to the stateless one, making late joiners miss state. Our single conflated latest-per-key map with connection-scoped expiry covers both jobs (late-joiner snapshot = the map, conflation under backpressure) without the client choosing between two primitives. Their two-primitive split and the CRDT cost that forced the 5-per-30s throttle are the divergence evidence: a full CRDT for latest-per-key-with-connection-expiry semantics is machinery beyond the job. Note their multi-node reality honestly: cross-node presence is where the CRDT earns it, and our multi-node ephemeral story is unwritten (plan keeps multi-node to Redis streams for durable channels; ephemeral fan-out across nodes is unspecified).

### Pagination / windowing
None. Topics are unwindowed firehoses. Broadcast Replay's `limit: 25` is the only window in the product. No analog to our keyset windows or searchIndex (plan, Pagination splits by layer).

### Client bundle + boot cost
`@supabase/realtime-js` 2.108.1: **54.7KB minified / 15.8KB gzip**, 2 dependencies ([bundlephobia API](https://bundlephobia.com/api/size?package=@supabase/realtime-js)). That buys channels, presence, postgres-changes bindings — no cursor machine, no outbox, no rebase, no persistence, no store. Useful calibration for the hobbyist bar (scenario-hobbyist: "small enough to not embarrass the CDN rung"): ~16KB gzip is what a bare channel client costs in production; our sync client does strictly more, and Zero's 232KB gzip remains the named non-budget. The realistic target sits between, much nearer realtime-js.

Boot: no local state to hydrate — boot cost is join latency (policy evaluation at join; complex RLS demonstrably raises connection time, per their own warning).

### Operational ceilings, incidents, redesigns
- WAL buildup → disk exhaustion → database crash → realtime outage (named in [discussion #21093](https://github.com/orgs/supabase/discussions/21093)).
- Replication lag > memory → slot reset → silent permanent gap.
- The Postgres Changes ceiling and the broadcast-from-database redesign (§3) — the flagship feature walked back to "simpler but does not scale."
- Channel leaks as the dominant client-side production failure: subscriptions created without `removeChannel` cleanup exhaust the 100-channels-per-connection quota silently ([third-party production report](https://www.agilesoftlabs.com/blog/2026/05/supabase-realtime-in-production-what), consistent with the quota design).
- Binary payloads silently dropped on old SDKs (their forward-compat gap; our §7 unknown-tolerance addresses the genre).
- Mitigation machinery visible in source: per-tenant everything, channel shutdown on rate breach, regional 2-node redundancy for replication slots, partition-drop retention.

## 5. Rationale Archaeology

1. **WALRUS, 2022** — accepted per-subscriber visibility checks as the price of correctness and optimized the constant factor: "rather than distribute checks, we focused on making the security check for each user on every change as fast as possible." Also: "If your policy is slow, all access to that table will be slow" and "The fastest security policy is one that doesn't exist." ([blog](https://supabase.com/blog/realtime-row-level-security-in-postgresql))
2. **Multiplayer GA, 2022** — Broadcast/Presence added because users were "sending all the mouse movements through their Postgres database." The durable path mispriced for ephemera; new tiers rather than new knobs. ([blog](https://supabase.com/blog/supabase-realtime-multiplayer-general-availability))
3. **Broadcast from Database, 2024** — the reversal completed: change delivery re-platformed onto the topic bus, authorization moved to join time, and the docs began steering users off Postgres Changes ("use Broadcast for most use cases"). The linear-in-subscribers cost was never beaten, only avoided. ([blog](https://supabase.com/blog/realtime-broadcast-from-database))
4. **Fire-and-forget defended, 2024-2026** — delivery guarantees repeatedly requested, repeatedly declined; the official healing pattern is refetch-on-reconnect. Then **Broadcast Replay** ships anyway (since/limit-25, 3-day window, private channels only) — the at-most-once posture softening exactly where data (not signals) flows through the bus. ([discussion](https://github.com/orgs/supabase/discussions/21093), [docs](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/realtime/broadcast))
5. **Infrastructure reversals** — stock distributed Erlang replaced by gen_rpc pooled transport for PubSub fan-out; cluster formation moved to Postgres-based libcluster strategy (use the database you already operate). Both are scale-driven replacements of Erlang defaults, undocumented outside source and a clustering blog post. ([application.ex](https://github.com/supabase/realtime/blob/main/lib/realtime/application.ex), [blog](https://supabase.com/blog/elixir-clustering-using-postgres))

| 12 | **adopt** (dev-surface) | Channel-leak telemetry | Leaked channels are their top client-side production failure; 100-channels-per-connection quota is the tripwire | Our refcounted component-lifecycle unsubscribe kills the class structurally, but module-scope subscribes outside components can still leak — a dev-mode held-channels counter (devtools surface) is cheap insurance | Hobbyist debugging story; enterprise long-lived SPAs | dev-only, tree-shaken |

## 7. Open Questions Carried Forward

1. **Re-auth re-gating** (candidate 4): does our `auth` refresh re-run channel permission gates, and what bounds revocation latency between subscribe and `server.revoke`? ws-protocol open question 8 is adjacent (revocation UX) but doesn't cover the refresh moment.
2. **Inbound ephemeral rate enforcement** (candidate 5): knob shape and default for the reference server — per-channel, per-client, or per-tenant? Supabase needed all three.
3. **Multi-node ephemeral state**: their presence CRDT solves cross-node latest-per-key; our ephemeral tier is specified single-node (in-memory map per channel instance). When multi-node lands (Redis streams for durable), what carries ephemeral — Redis pub/sub with local conflation maps?
4. **Quota enumeration in conformance suite**: which limit dimensions are protocol-visible (welcome caps?) vs deployment config — their named-error-code approach suggests at least naming them in the reason-code table.
5. **Watch their trajectory**: `pg-delta` dependency surfacing in June 2026 releases (possible new CDC engine), the undocumented Forum/Census regional-broadcast machinery, and whether Broadcast Replay grows toward a real cursor (limit raised, all sources, public channels) — each would sharpen or soften candidates 7 and 8.
